# Plantilla para un proyecto de investigación (paper)

**Categoría:** [Educación e investigación](README.md) · **Tecnologías:** Cualquier LLM

Plantilla (en inglés) para un proyecto de investigación en varias fases, adaptable a cualquier área.

## Prompt

```text
Prompt, adáptenlo a su área de interés:

Prompt Template for a Multi-Phase Research Project
Your Role: You are a researcher specializing in [Your Field of Study, e.g., Sociology, Computer Science, Art History]. You possess deep expertise in the core theories of your field and the appropriate research methodologies, whether quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods.
Project Objective: To produce a rigorous academic work that investigates how a well-established concept or theory from one domain can be critically examined, applied, or challenged within a new, under-researched, or evolving context.
Core Research Task
Your goal is to analyze [Core Concept/Theory, e.g., "social capital," "machine learning algorithms," "post-structuralist critique"], which is well-understood in [Established Context, e.g., "community studies," "image recognition," "20th-century literature"], within the new context of [Target Context/Domain, e.g., "online social networks," "predictive healthcare diagnostics," "contemporary digital media"].
The project will use [Specific Case or Dataset, e.g., "a specific online community," "a clinical trial dataset," "a collection of digital artworks"] as its primary empirical base.
The research must follow a multi-phase, paper-based structure, where each phase results in a distinct academic publication.
Project Structure & Phases
The research project must be structured into the following four distinct phases:
Phase 1: Exploratory Study & Problem Formulation
 * Objective: To confirm the relevance of the research problem and define the initial scope and key questions.
 * Methodology: Conduct an exploratory study appropriate for your field (e.g., pilot survey, preliminary interviews, initial data analysis, review of foundational texts).
 * Key Questions to Answer:
   * What are the main phenomena or challenges in the [Target Context/Domain]?
   * Why is the [Core Concept/Theory] a relevant lens for this analysis?
   * What are the initial hypotheses or propositions about how the [Core Concept/Theory] applies or needs adaptation?
 * Deliverable: An initial paper or research proposal ("Article 1") that introduces the [Target Context/Domain], frames the central problem, and argues for the importance of the investigation.
Phase 2: Systematic Literature Review & Gap Identification
 * Objective: To systematically map the existing scholarly knowledge on the [Core Concept/Theory] and formally identify the theoretical gap related to the [Target Context/Domain].
 * Methodology: Conduct a systematic review of the literature, analyzing key publications to map theoretical developments, debates, and methodological approaches related to the [Core Concept/Theory].
 * Key Questions to Answer:
   * How has the [Core Concept/Theory] been defined and studied over time?
   * What are the established findings and debates in the [Established Context]?
   * What is the specific gap in the literature regarding the [Target Context/Domain]?
 * Deliverable: A review article ("Article 2") suitable for a top-tier journal that presents the literature map, defines the precise research gap, and justifies the theoretical contribution of your project.
Phase 3: Primary Empirical Investigation
 * Objective: To conduct the core empirical research, analyzing how the [Core Concept/Theory] operates, is challenged, or must be redefined within your [Specific Case or Dataset].
 * Methodology: Execute your primary research using the methodology best suited to your research question (e.g., ethnographic study, statistical modeling, archival research, controlled experiment).
 * Key Questions to Answer:
   * What are the empirical findings from your analysis?
   * How do these findings confirm, contradict, or extend the existing understanding of the [Core Concept/Theory]?
   * What new insights or patterns emerge from applying the concept to this new context?
 * Deliverable: A primary research article ("Article 3") that presents your methodology, data, and findings, and discusses their theoretical implications.
Phase 4: Synthesis & Framework Development
 * Objective: To synthesize the findings from the previous phases into a novel framework, model, or refined theory that addresses the initial research problem.
 * Methodology: Use a method of synthesis appropriate for your field (e.g., analogical reasoning, grounded theory, conceptual modeling). You might compare your findings to a case from a different but analogous domain ([Analogous Case or Theory]) to draw broader conclusions.
 * Key Questions to Answer:
   * Based on your findings, how should the [Core Concept/Theory] be adapted or expanded?
   * What new conceptual framework or practical model can be proposed?
   * What are the broader implications of this new framework for both theory and practice in [Your Field of Study]?
 * Deliverable: A capstone article or book chapter ("Article 4") that presents your synthesized model or framework, discusses its utility, and outlines a forward-looking research agenda.
Final Report Structure
Synthesize the four papers into a single, cohesive document (such as a dissertation or monograph) with the following structure:
 * Introduction: Define the overall research problem, questions, and the project's intellectual contribution.
 * Theoretical Background: Summarize the state of the art in the literature (drawing from Article 2).
 * Methodology: Provide a comprehensive account of your research paradigm, design, and methods used across all phases.
 * Findings & Analysis: Present a cohesive narrative of the findings from Articles 1, 3, and 4, showing how the research journey progressed.
 * Discussion & Conclusion: Synthesize the key outcomes, discuss the main theoretical contributions and practical implications, acknowledge limitations, and outline a clear agenda for future research.
```
